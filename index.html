Federal & State Debt

Federal debt held by the public as % of GDP, plus state and local government debt.

Federal debt held by the public rose from 27% of GDP in 1970 to ~101% in 2026 — driven by recessions, wars, tax cuts, and pandemic relief. State and local debt remained relatively stable at 12–14% of GDP throughout the period.

Federal debt: OMB Historical Table 7.1 (debt held by the public). State & local: Federal Reserve Z.1 Financial Accounts. Note: the Fed series has a ~$776B break in early 2004 not explained by annual borrowing; levels before 2004 may be understated.

Annual Surplus / Deficit

Federal budget balance as a percentage of GDP. Surpluses shown in teal, deficits in terracotta.

The 1998–2001 surpluses are the only sustained positive stretch since 1969. The FY2026 deficit of 6.2% of GDP is roughly twice the 1970–2019 average of ~3.0%, and that average excludes the COVID years (FY2020: −14.9%).

Interest on the Debt

Net interest as a share of total federal outlays, FY1970–2026.

Net interest reached 14.2% of spending ($1.05T) in FY2026 — exceeding national defense for the third consecutive year. The all-time peak was 15.4% in FY1996. Zero interest-rate policy held costs artificially low from 2009 to 2022; the rapid rate hikes since have reversed that quickly.
Data & Sources

OMB Historical Table 3.1 (net interest as % of outlays) and Treasury FiscalData. The FRED net interest series definition changes after FY2014 and was not used.

Annual Funding: On Time?

Did Congress complete all regular appropriations before the fiscal year started (Oct 1)?

Congress finished its spending bills on time in roughly 4 of 50 fiscal years since FY1977 — and not once since FY1997. The government has operated on continuing resolutions or stop-gap measures in every fiscal year for nearly three decades.
Data & Sources

CRS R46595 and CRS R42647 (continuing resolutions). "On time" means all 12 annual appropriations bills signed before October 1. FY1977 was the first year of the current fiscal-year calendar.

Note: FY1970–76 preceded the current FY calendar. CRS reports indicate funding was late every year in that period but annual stop-gap counts are not fully documented.

Debt Ceiling Laws

Duration of each debt-limit law since 1970 — how many days until the next change?

58 of 77 debt-ceiling laws enacted since 1945 lasted less than a year. That share has fallen — 87% of laws through 1997 were short-term, vs. 45% since — but suspensions (which set no numerical limit) have become more common, making duration comparisons harder.
Data & Sources

CRS R41814 (history of debt-limit laws). Laws are classified as "suspension" (no dollar limit set, effective for a fixed period) vs. "raise" (new dollar ceiling). Neither classification as "full year" appears in the CRS source; the analysis defines ≥365 days as long-term.

Legislation Enacted

Total public laws enacted per two-year Congress, and average page length per law.

Fewer laws, but much longer ones. Congresses in the late 1960s–80s passed 600–900 laws per session. The 118th Congress (2023–24) passed 274. Meanwhile the average law grew from ~4 pages to ~24 pages — consolidating many smaller bills into large omnibus packages.
Data & Sources

National Archives public law counts. Page-length data through 121st Congress from Brookings Vital Statistics Table 6-4. Note: "fewer laws" partly reflects the shift to omnibus appropriations and larger authorization bills.

Partisan Control

House and Senate seat totals by party per Congress, plus which party held the White House.

House majority margins have collapsed — from +149 seats (D, 1977) to +5 (R, 2025). One party held the House, Senate, and White House in only 10 of 29 Congresses. Unified control did not reliably produce on-time budgets or longer-duration debt-ceiling agreements.
Data & Sources

House Historian party division data. Senate: Brookings Vital Statistics Table 1-20. White House: standard historical record. Note: party affiliation of independents (e.g., Bernie Sanders) follows caucus alignment.
